DFT vs experiment: formation enthalpies

Formation enthalpies of binary compounds read from published papers (literature data), plotted against the lowest-energy Materials Project and OQMD entry at the same composition. Both axes in kJ/mol-atom. The two DFT sources use different energy references and are shown side by side, never averaged. Hover or tap a point for its source, quote and page.

Typical causes: a per-formula value read as per atom, a table printing magnitudes with the sign given by the column heading, a high-temperature phase or polymorph missing from DFT, or a strongly correlated oxide where the DFT functional and corrections matter.

Read with care.
  • DFT formation energies are 0 K values without zero-point or thermal terms; experimental enthalpies are usually at 298 K (some at high temperature).
  • The Materials Project applies fitted energy corrections (notably to anions such as O, halides, S, N, H and to some transition-metal oxides; GGA/GGA+U mixing). OQMD's "standard" fit uses elemental reference energies fitted to experimental formation enthalpies. Neither is independent of experiment, and the two must not be averaged.
  • The lowest-energy DFT entry at the composition is used; the measured phase may be a different polymorph (the number of DFT entries at that composition is shown), or a disordered or off-stoichiometric phase.
  • Literature values include values cited from older work (often a compilation), model values when selected, and machine-extracted values: each point keeps its quote and page so it can be checked. Values printed without a sign or flagged magnitude-suspect are excluded by default.
  • Only binary compounds whose phase label gives a formula are matched; see dft_parity.json for the matching rules and exclusion counts.
